# Iranian Family Dinner Night

**Khoresh-e Fesenjoon** with **Persian-ish rice** (tahdig) and **Salad-e Shirazi**. This menu comes from **Samin Nosrat’s** Persian recipes — the stew and rice are her versions; the salad is the classic bright chopped salad that pairs with the meal.

Fesenjoon hails from the verdant northern Iranian hills and coast, where pomegranate and walnut trees grow. The stew braises bone-in chicken thighs in toasted walnuts, pomegranate juice, and pomegranate molasses. As the sauce cooks, it thickens and deepens to a dark brown similar to mole poblano. Thin it with water as needed so it stays silky, not overly sticky or syrupy. Sweet-sour pomegranate and walnut richness make this one of the most elegant dishes in Persian cuisine.

**Yield:** 6 servings (stew) · **Hands-on:** Plan for long, gentle simmering on the stew (~3 hours total)

## Full Menu

- **Main:** Khoresh-e Fesenjoon (walnut–pomegranate chicken stew)
- **Side:** Persian-ish rice with tahdig (crispy crust)
- **Salad:** Salad-e Shirazi
- **Optional:** Mast-o khiar or plain yogurt for serving

## Main: Khoresh-e Fesenjoon

*From Samin Nosrat’s essential Persian recipes (featured in NYT Cooking).*

### Ingredients

- 1 pound walnut pieces (about 4 cups)
- 6 to 8 bone-in chicken thighs (about 2¼ pounds)
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- Fine sea salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 4 cups cold-pressed pomegranate juice
- ¼ cup good-quality pomegranate molasses, plus more as needed
- ¼ teaspoon crumbled saffron threads
- 1 to 2 teaspoons granulated sugar (optional)
- Pomegranate seeds, for garnish

### Instructions

1. Heat oven to 350°F. Spread walnuts on a baking sheet and toast until golden brown inside, about 12 minutes. Cool to room temperature.
2. Remove chicken skin and discard or save for another use. Season chicken with turmeric, 2 teaspoons salt, and ½ teaspoon pepper. Set aside.
3. Set a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. When hot, add oil and brown chicken thighs in a single layer (batch if needed), 3–4 minutes per side. Remove chicken. Add onion with a pinch of salt; cook, stirring, until soft and golden brown, 16–18 minutes.
4. In a food processor, grind cooled walnuts as finely as possible without turning them into paste. (Or chop very fine by hand.) Add 2 cups pomegranate juice and ¼ cup molasses; blend until very smooth.
5. Add walnut paste and remaining pomegranate juice to the pot. Season with salt and partly cover. Cook over low heat, stirring frequently, about 2 hours. If the sauce sticks, stir in ¼ cup water. The sauce will thicken and turn deep dark brown.
6. Add saffron; taste and adjust with salt, pepper, and pomegranate molasses. The sauce should be delightfully sweet and sour — add up to 2 teaspoons sugar if too tart.
7. Add chicken to the sauce and simmer, uncovered, 45 minutes, or until chicken falls off the bone. Stir regularly; add splashes of water if needed to prevent burning. Skim pooled walnut oil from the top if you like. Taste, adjust seasoning, transfer to a serving dish, and garnish with pomegranate seeds.

**Serve hot** with Persian-ish rice, yogurt, and Shirazi salad.

## Side: Persian-ish Rice (With Tahdig)

*Samin Nosrat’s beginner-friendly gateway to crispy, golden rice — from Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat (Simon & Schuster, 2017).*

Every Persian cook treasures tahdig, the crisp crust at the bottom of the pot. This “Persian-ish” version uses boiled basmati and a skillet when you want tahdig without the full traditional multi-hour method.

### Ingredients

- 2 cups basmati rice
- Salt (for the boiling water — see step 3)
- 3 tablespoons plain yogurt
- 3 tablespoons butter
- 3 tablespoons neutral-tasting oil

### Instructions

1. Fill a large stockpot with 4 quarts water; bring to a boil over high heat.
2. Rinse rice in a bowl, swirling vigorously and changing the water at least five times, until the water runs much clearer. Drain.
3. When water boils, salt it heavily (about 6 tablespoons fine sea salt or a generous ½ cup kosher salt — the water should taste very salty). Add rice and stir.
4. Cook rice, stirring occasionally, until al dente, 6–8 minutes. Drain in a fine-mesh sieve and rinse with cold water to stop cooking. Drain well.
5. Remove 1 cup of the rice and mix with the yogurt.
6. Set a well-seasoned 10-inch cast iron skillet or nonstick pan over medium heat. Add oil and butter. When butter melts, spread the yogurt-rice mixture in the pan and level it. Mound the remaining rice toward the center. With a wooden spoon handle, poke 5–6 holes down to the bottom so steam can escape and a crust can form. You should see oil bubbling up the sides; add a little more oil if not.
7. Cook over medium heat, rotating the pan a quarter turn every 3–4 minutes, until a golden crust forms at the sides, 15–20 minutes. When the crust turns from pale amber to gold, reduce heat to low and cook 15–20 minutes more. The rice should be cooked through; err on slightly more browning if unsure (about 35 minutes total in the pan).
8. Run a spatula along the edges. Tip out excess fat from the bottom if needed, then flip onto a platter. Serve immediately.

**If it doesn’t unmold in one piece:** Scoop out the rice, chip out the tahdig in pieces, and serve — still delicious.

## Salad: Salad-e Shirazi

Bright, refreshing chopped salad — like a Persian pico de gallo. No lettuce, very crunchy. Toddlers often love the tiny diced pieces. Chop ahead; dress right before serving.

**Ingredients (serves 4)**

- 4 Persian cucumbers (or 1 English cucumber), finely diced
- 3–4 Roma tomatoes, finely diced (seeded for less juice if you prefer)
- ½ small red onion, finely diced (soak in cold water 10 minutes for a milder bite)
- ¼ cup chopped fresh herbs (parsley, cilantro, and/or mint)
- **Dressing:** 3 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, juice of 1–2 limes (or lemons), 1 tsp dried mint, salt and pepper to taste

### Instructions

1. Chop vegetables and herbs; store separately in the fridge if making ahead.
2. Just before serving, toss with dressing.

**Enjoy your Iranian Family Dinner Night!** The stew rewards patience — start it early, make rice and salad closer to serving, and finish with yogurt on the side.
