# Swiss Family Dinner Night

**Cozy alpine feast** — cheese fondue to start, then Zürcher Geschnetzeltes (creamy mushroom sauce) with crispy rösti. We made the main with **pork tenderloin** instead of veal; it works beautifully. About 30–45 minutes for the main once prep is done.

## Full Menu

- **Starter:** Cheese fondue
- **Main:** Zürcher Geschnetzeltes with pork tenderloin (classic recipe uses veal)
- **Side:** Rösti + fresh green salad (mixed greens, apple slices, walnuts, light vinaigrette)

---

## Starter: Cheese Fondue

Classic Swiss-style fondue for sharing at the table. Great with bread cubes, boiled baby potatoes, and apple slices.

**Ingredients (serves 4)**

- 400–500 g (14–18 oz) mixed Swiss cheeses (Gruyère + Emmental, or a fondue blend)
- 1 clove garlic, halved
- 1 tbsp cornstarch or flour
- 1 cup dry white wine (Chasselas or any Swiss white)
- 1–2 tsp lemon juice
- Pinch of nutmeg (optional)
- Kirsch (cherry brandy), optional splash
- Cubed crusty bread, boiled potatoes, and apple for dipping

**Instructions**

1. Toss grated cheese with cornstarch.
2. Rub the fondue pot with cut garlic; discard garlic or leave in for flavor.
3. Warm wine over medium-low heat. Add cheese in handfuls, stirring until melted and smooth.
4. If too thick, add a splash more wine; if too thin, stir in more cheese. Season with lemon juice, salt, and pepper. Add kirsch if using.
5. Keep warm over a low flame or fondue burner. Serve immediately with dippers.

## Main: Zürcher Geschnetzeltes (Zurich-Style Strips in Creamy Mushroom Sauce)

Classic recipe for veal strips; we used **pork tenderloin** sliced thin. Serves 4. Pairs with rösti and salad.

**Total time:** ~30–45 minutes

### Ingredients (Meat & Sauce)

- 400–500 g (14–18 oz) veal fillet or cutlets, thinly sliced into strips (freeze briefly for easier slicing; **we used pork tenderloin** — chicken or beef also work)
- 2–3 tbsp butter (divided)
- 1–2 tbsp olive oil or neutral oil
- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- 1–2 garlic cloves, minced
- 400–500 g (14–18 oz) white or cremini mushrooms, thinly sliced
- 125–150 ml (½ cup) dry white wine (Chasselas or any Swiss white)
- 200–250 ml (¾–1 cup) heavy cream (double cream)
- 100–125 ml (½ cup) chicken or beef stock
- 1 tbsp cornstarch or flour (mixed with a little water for thickening, optional)
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- 1–2 tsp lemon juice or zest (for brightness)
- Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

**Optional traditional touches:** A splash of Worcestershire sauce or a bit of paprika.

### Instructions

1. **Prep the meat:** Pat the strips dry and lightly dust with flour or cornstarch if desired (helps with browning and thickening). Season with salt and pepper.
2. **Brown the meat:** Heat 1 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Working in batches, sear strips quickly for 1–2 minutes per side until just browned but still tender. Remove to a plate. Don’t overcook — brief sear keeps pork or veal tender.
3. **Cook the vegetables:** In the same pan, add the remaining butter. Sauté onion and garlic 2–3 minutes until softened. Add mushrooms; cook 5–7 minutes until they release liquid and start to brown.
4. **Build the sauce:** Deglaze with white wine, scraping up browned bits. Let bubble and reduce by half (1–2 minutes). Stir in stock and cream. Simmer gently 3–5 minutes until the sauce starts to thicken.
5. **Finish:** Return the meat and any juices to the pan. Simmer 1–2 minutes to warm through and coat in sauce. If too thin, add a cornstarch slurry (1 tbsp cornstarch + 1 tbsp water) and stir until thickened. Season with salt, pepper, and lemon juice. Stir in parsley at the end.
6. **Serve:** Plate with crispy rösti and a fresh green salad.

### Tips for Success

- **Don’t overcook the meat** — It toughens quickly. A quick sear is key.
- **Sauce consistency** — Creamy but pourable, not too thick. Adjust with more stock or cream if needed.
- **Make-ahead** — Prep components in advance, but combine and heat just before serving for best texture.
- **Substitutions** — Lighter version: half cream and half milk, or more stock. Vegetarian: swap meat for mushrooms or plant-based strips.

## Side: Rösti (Swiss Potato Pancake)

Crispy shredded potato cakes — the classic partner for geschnetzeltes.

**Ingredients (serves 4)**

- 4 large russet or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled
- 2 tbsp butter or oil (plus more for frying)
- Salt and pepper

**Instructions**

1. Grate potatoes on the large holes of a box grater. Squeeze out excess moisture in a clean towel.
2. Season with salt and pepper; mix well.
3. Heat butter or oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-high. Press potato into one or two thick rounds (or individual cakes).
4. Cook 5–7 minutes per side until deep golden and crisp. Drain on paper towels if needed.
5. Serve hot alongside the geschnetzeltes.
